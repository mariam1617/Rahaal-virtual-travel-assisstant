# Egypt Travel Advisories and Safety Guidance

## 1. Regional Risks

This section covers regions of Egypt for which the FCDO has specific advice.

Travelers should also read the FCDO's overall travel advice and safety and security advice.

- Overall travel advice: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/egypt/warnings-and-insurance
- Safety and security: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/egypt/safety-and-security

### 1.1 Egypt–Libya Border

The FCDO advises against all travel within 20 km of the Egypt–Libya border, except for the town of El Salloum, where it advises against all but essential travel.

There are active military operations targeting criminal activity in this area.

### 1.2 North Sinai

The FCDO advises against all travel to North Sinai Governorate due to continuing criminal activity and terrorist attacks on police and security forces that have resulted in deaths.

Related guidance:
https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/egypt/safety-and-security#terrorism-in-egypt

### 1.3 Northern Part of South Sinai

The FCDO advises against all but essential travel to the northern part of South Sinai Governorate, beyond the St Catherine–Nuweibaa road, except for the coastal areas along the west and east of the peninsula.

The advice is due to criminal activity and terrorist attacks on police and security forces that have resulted in deaths.

If traveling in a 4-wheel-drive vehicle from mainland Egypt through the Suez crossing, travelers must obtain a permit from the Travel Permits Department of the Egyptian Ministry of the Interior.

Activities outside resort areas should be booked through approved agents or tour operators.

Travel to Red Sea tourist areas in the southern part of the Sinai Peninsula is generally considered low risk. Security forces carry out routine checks on vehicles and at airports to protect resorts such as Sharm El Sheikh, Hurghada, and Marsa Alam.

Related guidance:
https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/egypt/safety-and-security#terrorism

### 1.4 Eastern Part of Ismailiyah Governorate

The FCDO advises against all but essential travel to parts of Ismailiyah Governorate east of the Suez Canal due to ongoing military operations against terrorists and criminals.

Egyptian authorities at checkpoints may prevent onward travel to this region.

Related guidance:
https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/egypt/safety-and-security#terrorism

### 1.5 Western Desert

The FCDO advises against all but essential travel to the area west of the Nile Valley and Nile Delta regions due to risks from criminal activity and military operations, except for the following locations and routes:

- Luxor, Qina, the Valley of the Kings, Aswan, and Abu Simbel.
- Faiyum Governorate.
- Coastal areas between the Nile Delta and Marsa Matruh.
- The Marsa Matruh–Siwa road.
- The oasis town of Siwa.
- Giza Governorate northeast of Bahariya Oasis.
- The White Desert and Black Desert.
- The oasis towns of Bahariya, Farafra, Dakhla (Mut), and Kharga.
- The road between Giza and Farafra and the area within 50 km on either side of this road. The FCDO advises against all but essential travel on the road between Bahariya and Siwa.
- The road between Farafra, Dakhla (Mut), and Kharga.
- The road between Kharga and Baris.
- The road between Baris and Luxor.

Tourists entering the Western Desert areas of Bahariya Oasis, Farafra, the White Desert, and Black Desert must apply for permits in advance and use an official guide.

Phone reception is limited and medical facilities are poor. Travelers should monitor travel advice and follow guidance from official guides and local authorities.

The FCDO advises against all but essential travel on the road between Al Bawiti and Siwa, including the desert areas near the start of the road in Bahariya. The road is closed and there are military checkpoints.

Travelers going to the southwest corner of Egypt near the border with Sudan or Libya need a permit from the Ministry of the Interior. Bandits and armed groups operate in these areas.

Permit information:
https://emoves.moi.gov.eg/en

Travelers should be extremely cautious in all border areas.

### 1.6 Hala’ib Triangle and Bir Tawil Trapezoid

The FCDO advises against all but essential travel to the Hala’ib Triangle and Bir Tawil Trapezoid.

The Hala’ib Triangle remains disputed territory between Egypt and Sudan. British nationals requiring urgent UK government support in the Hala’ib Triangle should contact the British Embassy in Cairo.

The Bir Tawil Trapezoid is unclaimed by either Egypt or Sudan. British nationals requiring urgent UK government support in this area should contact the FCDO in the last country they traveled through.

British Embassy in Cairo:
https://www.gov.uk/world/organisations/british-embassy-cairo

FCDO contact:
https://www.contact.service.csd.fcdo.gov.uk/home

## 2. Warnings and Insurance

**Source updated:** 27 October 2026

The latest update concerns regional tensions.

Travel insurance could be invalidated if travelers travel against FCDO advice.

### 2.1 Regional Tensions

The context in the Middle East remains deeply unpredictable, and the risk of sudden escalation is high.

Since conflict began in early 2026, Iran has stated its intention to target locations associated with the US and Israel, including organizations, businesses, facilities, and institutions. There have been strikes and retaliatory attacks by Iran and others across the region against civilian and US military infrastructure.

### 2.2 Conflict in Israel and Palestine

International borders in Israel and Palestine could close at short notice, including the Taba border crossing between Israel and Egypt.

Travelers should check with local authorities and read the travel advice for Israel and Palestine before attempting to cross.

- Israel: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/israel
- Palestine: https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/palestine

#### Entering Egypt from Gaza

The Rafah border crossing is currently closed. If it reopens, Israel, Egypt, and the authorities in Gaza may determine who is permitted to cross and when.

British nationals in Gaza who want to leave should consult the relevant Israel and Palestine travel advice.

British nationals concerned about friends or family, or needing UK government support, can contact the British Embassy in Cairo.

## 3. Safety and Security

### 3.1 Terrorism

The source states that there is a high global threat of terrorist attacks affecting UK interests and British nationals, including from groups and individuals who view the UK and British nationals as targets.

Travelers should remain aware of their surroundings, keep up to date with local media reports, and follow local authorities' advice. They should be vigilant in crowds and large gatherings.

Potential targets listed in the source include:

- Egyptian security forces.
- Religious sites.
- Large public gatherings.
- Places visited by foreign nationals.

The source states that Egyptian authorities maintain a significant security presence and that additional measures are in place at tourist sites.

Examples of past attacks listed in the source:

- In 2024, one Israeli-Canadian national was killed in a shooting attack in Alexandria.
- In 2023, two Israeli tourists and an Egyptian tour guide were killed in a shooting attack in Alexandria.
- In 2022, four police officers and one insurgent were killed in an attack on a police checkpoint in Ismailiyah City.

### 3.2 Political Situation

The source describes Egypt's political environment as restrictive.

Travelers are advised to avoid large gatherings, demonstrations, and protests because the situation may change quickly and without warning.

The source states that foreign nationals involved in political activity or activities critical of the government, including negative social media comments, may be at risk of detention.

### 3.3 Crime

The source describes the crime rate as generally low and advises visitors to take sensible precautions.

Tourist police telephone number listed by the source: 126.

The source states that crimes should be reported before leaving Egypt because prosecution may otherwise be difficult.

### 3.4 Sexual Assault and Harassment

The source reports sexual assaults involving foreign nationals, including cases involving minors. It states that many reported cases occurred in Red Sea tourist resorts and were often committed by someone the victim already knew, including hotel workers and excursion staff.

The source also reports cases where hotel staff discouraged reporting incidents to local police.

Female travelers are advised to exercise caution when traveling alone, particularly at night and in taxis.

The source recommends the following precautions:

- Do not leave children unaccompanied.
- Avoid allowing hotel staff to enter a room when alone; try to ensure a friend or relative is present.
- Avoid traveling alone in a taxi when possible. If this cannot be avoided, share the driver and trip details and live location with a friend.
- Research service and excursion providers and use reputable providers.
- Avoid sharing personal contact details or social media details.

### 3.5 Protecting Belongings

The source states that pickpocketing is rare in Egypt but recommends ordinary precautions.

Travelers may keep separate photocopies of their passport, visa, and flight ticket.

If a passport is lost or stolen, travelers should notify the police immediately and obtain a police report. The report is needed to transfer visa stamps to a replacement passport.

### 3.6 Scams and Touts at Tourist Sites

Travelers should be cautious in tourist areas, where scammers and touts sometimes target foreign nationals.

## 4. Laws and Cultural Differences

### 4.1 Religion and Ramadan

The source states that Egypt is predominantly an Islamic country and advises travelers to respect local traditions, customs, laws, and religions, especially during Ramadan and when visiting religious areas.

The source states that encouraging people to convert to Christianity or another religion is illegal.

Ramadan dates vary by year and country. Travelers should check the dates before traveling.

During Ramadan, the source advises travelers to consider the following:

- Eating, drinking, or smoking in public during daylight hours may be culturally insensitive.
- Avoid loud music, dancing, or swearing in public.
- Check shop and restaurant opening hours.
- Hotels and restaurants serving food or drink during fasting hours may separate non-fasting guests, for example with screens.
- Follow local dress codes.
- Driving may be erratic, particularly around dusk.
- Be patient and show tolerance.

Travelers can seek additional advice from tour guides, hotels, or business contacts.

### 4.2 Personal Identification

The source states that police check identification regularly and may ask to see a passport.

Travelers may be detained if they cannot show their identification and visa, or a digital or paper copy.

### 4.3 Dress Code and Public Behavior

The source advises modest dress, particularly in rural areas, mosques, and souqs (markets).

Public displays of affection by couples are described as frowned upon, while holding hands is generally considered acceptable. Behavior accepted in tourist resort areas may not be accepted elsewhere.

### 4.4 Alcohol Laws

The source states that drinking alcohol outside licensed restaurants, bars, private homes, or certain tourist resorts is illegal and can lead to arrest.

### 4.5 Illegal Drugs

The source describes possession, use, or trafficking of illegal drugs as a serious offense that can lead to life imprisonment or the death penalty, even for small amounts.

The source also states that khat is illegal in Egypt.

### 4.6 Photography and Filming Restrictions

The source states that a permit is required to enter Egypt with professional photography or film equipment, including photography umbrellas, artificial outdoor lighting equipment, and equipment that occupies or blocks public roads.

The source states that taking or sharing photographs that could be considered damaging to the country's image is illegal.

The source's summary of Ministry of Tourism regulations states that written permission from an Egyptian citizen is required before photographing them and that photographing children is prohibited. It also advises against photographing officials without consent.

Photographs of or near military property are prohibited, including the Suez Canal.

The source lists the following as locations where photography is sensitive:

- Embassies.
- Government buildings and infrastructure.
- Religious buildings.

Travelers who are unsure should obtain permission before taking photographs.

Related source:
https://egymonuments.gov.eg/en/news/egyptian-government-decided-to-allow-egyptians-and-tourists-to-take-photographs-for-free/

### 4.7 Drones

The source states that importing, using, or producing drones in Egypt requires prior permission from the Egyptian Ministry of Defence.

Unauthorized use or import may result in a prison sentence of up to seven years or a fine of EGP 5,000 to EGP 50,000.

Related source:
https://www.mod.gov.eg/ModWebSite/Default.aspx

### 4.8 LGBT+ Travelers

The source states that same-sex sexual activity is not explicitly criminalized in Egypt but that Egyptian authorities have prosecuted LGBT+ people under “debauchery” laws, including people flying rainbow flags at public events.

The source describes public acceptance of same-sex relationships as limited and warns that same-sex couples showing affection in public may receive negative attention.

It advises caution when sharing sexual content or discussing sexual acts on social media, particularly acts considered illegal.

The source also states that travelers may be denied entry if their gender expression or physical appearance does not match the sex marker in their passport or passport photograph.

Related guidance:
https://www.gov.uk/guidance/lesbian-gay-bisexual-and-transgender-foreign-travel-advice

### 4.9 Child Custody Law

The source states that Egyptian family law differs considerably from UK law, particularly regarding child custody.

Related guidance:
https://www.gov.uk/guidance/international-parental-child-abduction

## 5. Outdoor Activities and Adventure Tourism

### 5.1 Insurance for Adventure Activities

Travelers should ensure that their travel insurance, or the tour or dive company, covers the cost of air or sea rescue. The source states that rescue can cost more than US$4,000 per hour and that Egyptian authorities will only begin rescue operations when payment is guaranteed.

The source advises booking excursions through a resort, approved agents, or tour operators.

Insurance guidance:
https://www.gov.uk/guidance/foreign-travel-insurance

### 5.2 Hot Air Balloons

The source advises travelers to speak to their tour company before booking a hot air balloon flight.

Some UK tour operators have stopped selling balloon flights because they could not verify safety standards.

### 5.3 Watersports and Cruises

The source reports incidents involving British nationals on diving boats and tours in the Red Sea, including fatalities.

Safety and insurance standards among diving operators and boats vary. Some operators may not provide adequate safety or insurance standards.

The Marine Accident Investigation Branch published guidance about liveaboard vessels in February 2025. The source recommends booking liveaboard dive holidays through reputable vendors who can provide assurance about boat safety standards.

Before departure, travelers should request a thorough safety briefing from the crew.

The source also recommends:

- Observe safety standards on board and exercise caution.
- Book through a tour representative or reputable operator where possible.
- Do not dive beyond the depth limit in the insurance policy, as this invalidates coverage.

Shark attacks are described as rare, but attacks on tourists in the Red Sea region have occurred. Travelers should monitor updates from local authorities or their tour operator.

Related guidance:
https://www.gov.uk/government/news/safety-bulletin-12025-published

Watersports safety:
https://www.rospa.com/leisure-water-safety/water/advice/watersports-abroad

### 5.4 Quad Bikes and Desert Visits

The source reports several serious quad bike accidents involving British nationals in resort areas.

Travelers should take precautions and always wear a crash helmet. Safety standards vary considerably.

## 6. Transport Risks

### 6.1 Road Travel

The source advises travelers planning to drive in Egypt to consult its guidance on driving abroad.

For travelers using a UK driving licence, the source states that both the 1949 version of the International Driving Permit (IDP) and the UK driving licence must be carried in the car.

The source states that an IDP cannot be purchased outside the UK, so it should be obtained before travel.

Travelers can drive in Egypt using a UK licence and an IDP for up to six months. Those planning to stay longer must apply for an Egyptian driving licence.

Drivers and front-seat passengers must wear seatbelts.

Driving under the influence of alcohol or drugs is a serious offense that can result in a fine and possible imprisonment.

The source states that importing a vehicle into Egypt may not be allowed. Temporary vehicle entry requires a carnet de passage from the Automobile Association.

### 6.2 Driving Conditions

The source reports that accidents are common because of variable road conditions, dangerous driving, and poor traffic law enforcement.

It advises against driving outside main cities and resorts at night and recommends appropriate insurance.

### 6.3 Off-Road Travel

Travelers going off-road should use a qualified guide and obtain a permit from the Ministry of the Interior.

Permit information:
https://emoves.moi.gov.eg/en

### 6.4 Rail and Bus Travel

The source reports that serious bus crashes in recent years have caused many deaths, including among tourists.

It also reports fatal train collisions and derailments.

### 6.5 River and Sea Travel

The source reports accidents involving Red Sea ferries and Nile cruises caused by overcrowding and poor safety standards.
